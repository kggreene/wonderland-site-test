The Last Leaf

Draft story, written to fill this space. Nana's own version is coming.

When all the other leaves had fallen, one leaf stayed on the big tree. It was red with a streak of gold down the middle, and it held on to the very end of the longest branch with its thin dark stem, and it would not let go.

The wind tried. "Come down," said the wind, "everyone else has." The leaf only fluttered and held on tighter.

The charms from Castle Charms drifted past it, and the pink parachute floated by, with two cats on top, and the dragon walking underneath looked up and waved. Still the leaf held on. It liked the view.

Every afternoon the girl came and sat under the tree and looked up at it. She did not ask it to come down. She just told it the news: who had been to tea, how fast Bus Driver Pig had gone that day, what the Caterpillar had asked.

Then one morning, very early, the leaf let go. It did not fall the way the others had. It turned over once, slowly, and came straight down into the girl's open hands, as if it had been waiting for her all along.

She took it home and pressed it flat in her notebook, between two pages of drawings. It is still there.
